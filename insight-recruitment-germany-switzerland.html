One region, different talent markets.

Why a search in Switzerland cannot simply be copied and pasted into Germany.

By MuJobs3 min readRecruitment practice
IN THIS ARTICLELocalise the brief, not just the job advertSearch with more than one vocabularyDiscuss practical conditions earlyCompare relevance within each market

Germany and Switzerland share a language across large parts of the market and often use similar job titles. That can make a cross-border search look easier than it is.

Company structures, terminology, salary expectations, geography, commuting patterns and the importance of local networks can change the candidate pool. The target companies, title variations and search radius therefore need to change with the market.

DACH is useful as a commercial label. For sourcing, it is better treated as connected but distinct talent markets. The search becomes stronger when the map reflects those differences.

01

Localise the brief, not just the job advert

Recruitment in Germany and Switzerland can share a sourcing framework without sharing an identical brief. Clarify where the work happens, how the company is organised and which relationships the role needs. A German-language advert alone does not explain the market context. For cross-border searches, agree whether the business genuinely supports relocation, commuting or remote work before including those candidates in the target pool.

02

Search with more than one vocabulary

Relevant people may describe the same work using German, English or company-specific titles. In property recruitment, Projektentwicklung, Real Estate Development and a project-lead title may overlap but are not identical. Build title variations into the research, then use responsibilities to qualify the results. The language of a profile should not be confused with the language capability needed for the job; confirm the latter through the appropriate conversation.

03

Discuss practical conditions early

Location, travel, compensation structure and working arrangements can determine whether a conversation continues. Explain the conditions the employer has actually agreed rather than promising flexibility that has not been confirmed. Where employment or cross-border questions arise, use the employer’s qualified advisers for the specific situation. Sourcing can identify interest, but it should not make assumptions about a person’s eligibility or practical ability to take up the role.

04

Compare relevance within each market

A cross-border market map should show why each company and career path belongs in the search. Keep market observations separate from assumptions and revise the brief as conversations provide evidence. One location may offer relevant technical experience while another offers stronger local relationships. Make that trade-off explicit. A useful DACH shortlist helps the employer understand these differences rather than flattening them into a single list of job titles.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Bring one live role to the conversation.

Take the questions in this article and apply them to the position you are trying to fill. Write down what is known, what is assumed and which conversation would resolve the most important uncertainty. A clearer brief is a practical first step towards a more relevant search.
