Your key role is still open—and the business is running the search? How MuJobs adds focused recruiting capacity

When managers research profiles, write outreach and screen CVs alongside their day jobs, the vacancy becomes a capacity problem. A clearly divided search process gets hiring moving again.

The role matters, but nobody can run the search consistently. HR coordinates several vacancies, the hiring manager researches between customer meetings and specialists repeatedly review profiles. The position remains open while decisions, projects or customer work continue to wait for the missing expertise.

MuJobs adds capacity exactly at this bottleneck. The employer retains the professional and business decision. MuJobs takes on the labour-intensive preparation: clarify the role, research the target market, approach relevant people personally, check the essential experience and build a reasoned shortlist.

1. Recognise when the vacancy has become a capacity problem

Not every open role needs external support. The question is whether the internal team can run the search with the required quality and consistency. Half an hour of research between other tasks rarely creates a credible market view; irregular follow-up allows promising conversations to go cold.

MuJobs therefore starts with a short process diagnosis. We clarify the role and examine what search work is already happening, where time is being lost and who owns the professional decision. External recruiting capacity is then applied where it genuinely relieves the internal process.

  • The role is business-critical but has no dedicated internal search owner.
  • HR or the business researches only intermittently and without a complete market view.
  • Profiles are reviewed repeatedly because essential criteria are not prioritised.
  • Interested people wait too long for feedback or an interview date.
  • The function needs a relevant shortlist but cannot scale outreach itself.

2. Keep the professional decision inside and outsource focused search work

An effective partnership does not replace the hiring manager. The company must decide what the new hire will own, which conditions apply and who should be employed. MuJobs organises the search so those decisions are based on better-prepared conversations.

Before the search starts, we agree a clear division of work. Everyone knows who confirms criteria, who works the market and when feedback is due.

    3. How MuJobs adds recruiting capacity

    Additional capacity does not mean sending the largest possible number of profiles. MuJobs works from the assignment to the candidate market. The brief defines which experience matters; research looks beyond identical titles; personal outreach explains the specific connection to the role.

    During the first conversation, we establish whether interest and relevant experience broadly align. The shortlist then makes evidenced fit and open questions visible. The hiring manager can focus time on conversations that provide a real basis for a decision.

    • Clarify the role: agree ownership, outcomes and essential experience.
    • Structure the market: map relevant titles, employers, locations and adjacent fields.
    • Approach personally: explain the connection between profile, assignment and reason to move.
    • Qualify what matters: discuss motivation, experience, location and essential conditions.
    • Shortlist with evidence: present relevant profiles with reasoning and open points to test.

    4. Timely feedback is part of search delivery

    More research alone will not fill a vacancy. If criteria change during the search or introduced profiles sit without feedback, the process loses credibility. A fixed feedback rhythm is therefore part of working with MuJobs.

    We identify one decision-maker and agree how profiles will be assessed. Market responses are treated as evidence rather than promises: Is the search radius too narrow? Is important role information missing? Is a requirement truly essential? Any change is documented and then applied consistently to all profiles.

    • A named professional decision-maker is available for role questions.
    • Introduced profiles are assessed against the same prioritised criteria.
    • Feedback is specific and provided within an agreed timeframe.
    • Interview availability and next steps are predictable before outreach begins.
    • Changes to the search are reasoned and incorporated transparently.

    5. Use labour-market evidence as context, not a blanket explanation

    Germany's Federal Employment Agency identified shortages in 157 occupational categories for 2025, while also stressing that the evidence does not support a general labour shortage. Occupation, qualification and region matter. Switzerland's Federal Statistical Office reported year-on-year growth in vacancies and increasing difficulty recruiting qualified people in the second quarter of 2026, while publishing the data by sector and education level.

    This is not a blanket excuse for a difficult vacancy. A concrete role needs a concrete market analysis. MuJobs uses official labour-market evidence as context, then tests actual reach through the search brief, the relevant target market and responses to personal outreach.

      6. Decision check: do you need additional recruiting capacity?

      • The vacancy is delaying specific decisions, projects or customer work.
      • The internal team cannot sustain market research and personal outreach.
      • The role and its principal conditions can be clarified with a decision-maker.
      • Relevant profiles are likely to sit outside the active applicant market.
      • The hiring manager can provide focused interviews and timely feedback.

      If these points apply, your professional team does not need to build a complete direct-search operation alongside its core work. MuJobs can structure the search, work the relevant market and prepare a reasoned shortlist. The next step is a short conversation about the role, the search so far and the bottleneck in the internal process.
