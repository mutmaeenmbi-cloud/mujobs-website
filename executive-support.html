01

Where the search becomes difficult

These roles often carry responsibilities that a standard job description misses: deciding what needs attention, protecting other people’s time, managing sensitive information and keeping work moving between teams. A strong CV alone does not establish whether the person will thrive in your particular environment.

03

The evidence behind a relevant shortlist

Our starting questions connect the role to the work. The final assessment criteria are agreed for your particular brief, rather than applied as a generic checklist.

  • The pace of work, stakeholder mix and level of autonomy.
  • Organisation, prioritisation and communication in concrete situations.
  • Relevant tools, discretion and responsibility across processes.
04

Map the market. Make the connection.

We start with the actual working week: who the person supports, which decisions they own and where the current bottlenecks sit. Comparable responsibilities may appear under different titles. Structured conversations make it easier to assess judgement, reliability and fit without reducing the search to a checklist of tools.

What the search can include

  • A focused briefing and role-specific market map.
  • Relevant profile research and personal candidate outreach.
  • Initial qualification, follow-ups and interview coordination.
  • A shortlist with clear reasoning and visible open questions.

Start with the brief

Share the responsibility you need to fill, the location, the team structure and what has made the role hard to close. Together, we can distinguish what is essential from what is flexible and agree a search that fits the business.
