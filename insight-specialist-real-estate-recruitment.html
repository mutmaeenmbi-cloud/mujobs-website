Hiring inside a small specialist talent market.

How geography, project type, ownership and adjacent experience shape a real-estate search.

By MuJobs3 min readRecruitment practice
IN THIS ARTICLEDescribe the project before describing the personLook for adjacent experience with a clear connectionSeparate local knowledge from location preferenceMake the shortlist a decision document

Real-estate recruitment becomes difficult quickly because a familiar title can describe very different work. Two project developers can have completely different exposure depending on their employer, projects and stage of development.

A useful search goes below the title: acquisition, feasibility, approvals, development concepts, commercial evaluation, project scale and whether the experience comes from a developer, investor, planner or execution environment.

Geography matters too. Local relationships with municipalities, owners and stakeholders can be part of a candidate’s value. Strong shortlists combine role evidence, project context, geography and adjacent experience rather than treating matching titles as interchangeable.

01

Describe the project before describing the person

For Immobilien recruitment, begin with the work that is waiting. Is the business acquiring a site, taking a concept through approvals, coordinating delivery or preparing units for sale? What asset type and project scale are involved? A development brief should distinguish between coordinating consultants and owning the commercial case. These details make it possible to identify relevant experience even when a candidate uses a different title.

02

Look for adjacent experience with a clear connection

A narrow market does not mean every requirement should be relaxed. It means deciding which evidence matters most. An architect may be relevant for Projektentwicklung if they have also handled feasibility, stakeholders and commercial decisions. A broker may have strong buyer relationships but limited development responsibility. Explain the connection between past work and the new task rather than assuming that everyone in real estate is interchangeable.

03

Separate local knowledge from location preference

Local experience can matter because of relationships, market familiarity or the way projects are organised. But living nearby is not a substitute for that experience. Clarify whether the role requires an established regional network, regular site presence or simply a workable commute. Discuss travel and location early. Otherwise a technically strong shortlist can fail late for a practical reason that could have been clarified at the first conversation.

04

Make the shortlist a decision document

For each person, summarise project context, responsibilities, relevant scale and the questions still open. In a Leiter Vermarktung search, distinguish sales execution from pricing, positioning and team leadership. In project acquisition, clarify whether the person sourced opportunities, negotiated terms or supported someone else’s decision. The interview can then test specific evidence instead of repeating a chronological tour of the CV.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Bring one live role to the conversation.

Take the questions in this article and apply them to the position you are trying to fill. Write down what is known, what is assumed and which conversation would resolve the most important uncertainty. A clearer brief is a practical first step towards a more relevant search.
