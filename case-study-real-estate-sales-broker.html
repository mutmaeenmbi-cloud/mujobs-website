01

The hiring challenge

The strongest real-estate sales people are often defined by their market relationships and transaction history, not by a standard CV. A useful search therefore has to understand asset type, geography, buyer/seller network, mandate experience and evidence of taking deals from first conversation to close.

02

The search approach

mujobs would map relevant brokerages, developers and owner-side sales teams, then qualify people around deal evidence, local network, client ownership and the type of property they have actually sold.

Outreach would be personalised around the mandate and market rather than sent as a generic sales vacancy.

03

The outcome

The output is a focused pool of commercially credible people with evidence that matches the mandate — giving the client a stronger basis for interviews than title matching alone.

04

Understanding the role

For broker recruitment, a local network is meaningful only in relation to the mandate. Residential and commercial transactions, owner representation and buyer advisory can require different relationships and skills. A useful assessment connects the person’s deal history to the property type and client group the new role will serve.

Questions that make the brief more precise

  • Which asset types and geographic markets have they actually sold in?
  • How were mandates won and client relationships maintained?
  • What was their role in negotiation and closing?
05

Planning a similar search

Start with the work the new person will own, the business context and the constraints that cannot change. Separate essential experience from preferences. That gives the market mapping a clear direction and makes it easier to explain why someone belongs on the shortlist.

A similar title is only a research starting point. In an initial conversation, we would clarify the project or team, the practical conditions and the evidence you need to make a decision. The scope of sourcing, candidate qualification and interview coordination can then be agreed around the actual requirement.
