Does your role fit no standard category? How MuJobs recruits for hybrid positions

When a role combines technology, marketing, sales or operational ownership, a familiar job title often attracts the wrong profiles. MuJobs translates the actual work into a precise search field.

The company does not need a conventional replacement. It needs someone who combines several areas of ownership: product understanding and B2B sales, performance marketing and creative direction, or project development and commercial responsibility. Yet the vacancy still carries one familiar title. Applicants recognise only part of the work, while relevant profiles do not see the connection.

For these searches, MuJobs does not begin with a list of titles. We clarify the outcomes the person should own, which responsibilities genuinely belong together and which experience can transfer. This produces a practical brief, several relevant search directions and personal outreach that explains the unusual opportunity clearly.

1. Why one job title can make the search too narrow

Occupational titles are useful orientation, but they do not capture every combination of work. The European ESCO classification therefore connects occupations with knowledge, skills and competences; Germany's Federal Employment Agency also assigns core and additional competences to occupations in its competence catalogue.

For employers, the practical implication is clear: when a role has an unusual shape, the search should not depend only on an exact title. The relevant question is which responsibilities a person has already owned and which combination of skills the new assignment requires.

  • The title describes only one part of the actual responsibility.
  • Different sectors use different names for comparable work.
  • Some relevant people do not recognise themselves in the advert.
  • Unsuitable applications arrive because the title appears broader than the work.
  • Internal selection compares titles although concrete outcomes matter more.

2. MuJobs breaks the hybrid role into decision-relevant components

In the search briefing, MuJobs separates outcome ownership, daily responsibilities, essential knowledge and practical conditions. This shows which elements must sit with one person and which the existing team can complement.

Instead of keeping a long wish list, we prioritise the few pieces of evidence that should support a candidate introduction. The result is a profile broad enough to include adjacent careers and precise enough for a transparent shortlist.

    Before clarification
    • A familiar title with many unprioritised requirements.
    • No clear separation between essentials and learning areas.
    • Research limited to one sector or functional family.
    • CVs assessed by similarity rather than evidence.
    After the MuJobs briefing
    • Concrete outcomes and areas of ownership are defined.
    • Three to five central evidence points are prioritised.
    • Adjacent titles and sectors are included deliberately.
    • Open learning areas remain visible in the shortlist.

    3. One search profile opens several relevant talent markets

    MuJobs does not research only people with the supposedly perfect title. We map several directions: directly comparable roles, adjacent functions and profiles with transferable ownership. For each direction, we define which evidence makes the connection to the position credible.

    For a hybrid marketing and sales role, that evidence may include campaign ownership, pipeline contribution and management of external partners. For a technology-related commercial role, it may include product understanding, consultative selling and collaboration with technical teams.

    • Comparable responsibility under a different title.
    • Relevant experience from an adjacent sector.
    • Proven interface work between two functions.
    • A similar breadth of responsibility in a smaller company.
    • A development step in which only one clearly named element is new.

    4. Personal outreach explains the assignment, not just the title

    Passive candidates need to understand quickly why their experience may be relevant to an unusually shaped role. MuJobs therefore avoids a generic vacancy message. The approach connects one concrete part of the profile with the responsibility, business context and possible next step.

    In the initial conversation, we check interest, comparable ownership and essential conditions. The candidate introduction then shows which requirements are evidenced, which experience appears transferable and which question the employer should still test in the professional interview.

    • Which profile evidence prompted the contact?
    • Which particular responsibilities does the position combine?
    • Why could the move make professional sense?
    • Which conditions should be clarified early?
    • Which open question belongs in the next conversation?

    5. Check: is your hybrid position ready for the market?

    • The most important outcomes for the first twelve months are described.
    • It is clear which responsibilities must sit with one person.
    • Essentials are separated from preferences and learning areas.
    • Adjacent titles, sectors and career paths are explicitly allowed.
    • Interview questions test ownership and examples rather than title similarity.
    • Location, working model, compensation and decision authority are aligned.

    When a position combines several functions, it does not need an artificially perfect title. It needs a clear work profile and research that recognises relevant experience across several talent markets. MuJobs can handle the briefing, market mapping, personal outreach and qualified shortlisting. A hire cannot be guaranteed, but a more precise route to the market can be prepared.
