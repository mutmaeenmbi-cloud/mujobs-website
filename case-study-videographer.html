01

The hiring challenge

Videographer titles hide very different skill sets. The client needed someone with creative judgement as well as production capability: concept development, camera work, editing, storytelling and the ability to deliver content consistently for a commercial environment.

02

The search approach

mujobs treated the portfolio as evidence. The market was mapped around relevant creative profiles and candidates were reviewed for style, production range, commercial work and the ability to own more than one stage of the content process.

03

The outcome

59 relevant candidates were mapped into the search, creating a focused creative pipeline instead of a broad list of people who simply carried the Videographer title.

04

Understanding the role

Video production roles vary from camera operation to complete creative ownership. A portfolio-led search needs to establish which parts of each piece the candidate produced and how they work within real commercial constraints. Relevant evidence includes planning, shooting, editing, feedback handling and the ability to deliver consistently rather than one impressive showreel alone.

Questions that make the brief more precise

  • Which stages of production did they own on the portfolio examples?
  • How do they plan a shoot against time, budget and brand requirements?
  • How do they turn feedback into a finished, usable asset?
05

Planning a similar search

Start with the work the new person will own, the business context and the constraints that cannot change. Separate essential experience from preferences. That gives the market mapping a clear direction and makes it easier to explain why someone belongs on the shortlist.

A similar title is only a research starting point. In an initial conversation, we would clarify the project or team, the practical conditions and the evidence you need to make a decision. The scope of sourcing, candidate qualification and interview coordination can then be agreed around the actual requirement.
