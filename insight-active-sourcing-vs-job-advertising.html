Job ads create applicants. Search creates access.

When proactive market mapping becomes more useful than waiting for another application.

Recruitment starts with a clear brief.

A job advertisement shows you who is looking. It does not show you everyone who could be right for the role.

Proactive sourcing turns the brief into a market map: relevant companies, titles, locations, career paths and adjacent talent pools. The aim is not more messages. It is fewer, more relevant conversations with people the application funnel may never reach.

Applications still matter. But when the right people are not entering the funnel, waiting longer does not necessarily improve it. Search creates access to the market outside the inbox.

Match the channel to the hiring problem

Job advertising works by making an opportunity visible to people who are looking. Direct search works by identifying relevant people whether or not they are looking. Neither channel removes the need for a clear role, a credible proposition and an organised interview process. The decision should start with the problem: too few applications, too many unsuitable applications or a narrow combination of skills that rarely appears in one profile. Different problems call for different interventions.

Diagnose the funnel before increasing the budget

Review where the process is losing relevance. If good applicants never reach a first conversation, response speed or screening may be the issue. If most applicants lack an essential responsibility, the advert or targeting may be unclear. If a specialist role has remained open despite a reasonable campaign, a proactive market map can test whether the required combination exists. More exposure will not automatically fix a brief that combines several different jobs.

Use one scorecard across both channels

An inbound candidate and a sourced candidate should be assessed against the same evidence. Define the outcomes the hire must deliver, the experience that supports those outcomes and the points that can be learned. Record why each person is being introduced and what still needs to be checked. This gives the hiring manager a comparable shortlist and prevents enthusiasm about a particular company name from replacing a fair assessment.

Keep the handover simple

Agree who owns outreach, screening, interview scheduling and candidate updates before the search begins. A sourced candidate may need more context at the first meeting because they did not arrive through a detailed application. Share the same core role information and keep the interview steps proportionate. The goal is not to build parallel processes for every channel; it is to create a coherent experience from first contact to decision.
