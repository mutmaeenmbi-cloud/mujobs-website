Your job advert is live, but suitable applicants are missing: how active sourcing expands the candidate market

When a vacancy is visible but the right profiles do not apply, another posting is rarely enough. Employers need a clear search brief, focused market research and personal outreach.

The job advert is live. Applications arrive, but not the experience the team actually needs. Or there is almost no response. Meanwhile, colleagues absorb extra work, decisions are postponed and important projects wait for the missing expertise.

This does not automatically mean that suitable people do not exist. An advert mainly reaches people who are currently searching and find the posting. MuJobs adds focused market research and personal outreach to relevant specialists and leaders, including people who are not actively browsing job boards.

1. First identify why the advert is not reaching the right people

More reach does not solve an imprecise role brief. Before MuJobs starts direct outreach, we clarify what the new hire will actually own, which experience must be evidenced and which conditions are flexible.

We examine three possible causes: Is the role understandable? Does the offer fit the reachable market? Is the company searching across the right job titles, locations and adjacent sectors? This diagnosis prevents the same unclear search from simply being repeated on another channel.

  • Which outcomes will the person own in the role?
  • Which two or three experiences are essential, and what can be learned?
  • Which location, presence, language or compensation constraints shape the market?
  • Which alternative titles and adjacent sectors contain comparable experience?
  • Why should a relevant person discuss this specific opportunity?

2. Job advertising and active sourcing do different jobs

A job advert remains useful. It makes the vacancy publicly discoverable and gives active jobseekers a clear route to apply. It should therefore be precise, credible and understandable from the candidate's perspective.

Active sourcing begins where waiting is not enough. MuJobs turns the search brief into a market map, identifies relevant profiles and approaches selected people personally. The message explains why their particular background may fit the work instead of sending a generic mass message.

    Job advert
    • Reaches people who are actively searching or discover the posting.
    • Explains the role, employer and application route publicly.
    • Creates an inbound application flow that must be screened internally.
    Focused active sourcing with MuJobs
    • Maps the relevant market by experience, location and role.
    • Reaches selected people through personal outreach.
    • Checks interest and relevant experience before shortlisting.

    3. How MuJobs builds a focused search

    MuJobs connects five steps into a transparent search process. We first turn the hiring need into a clear brief. We then research the employers, functions and profiles that may form the relevant talent market.

    Outreach is tailored to the role and the person. Responses also provide market signals: unclear responsibilities, an overly narrow search radius or missing conditions become visible and can be discussed with the employer. Interested people are assessed against the agreed criteria before MuJobs creates a reasoned shortlist.

    • Clarify the role: agree ownership, essential criteria and flexible points.
    • Map the market: research relevant titles, employers, locations and adjacent fields.
    • Approach personally: explain the specific connection between profile and opportunity.
    • Qualify what matters: establish interest, experience, location and open questions.
    • Shortlist with evidence: present relevant profiles with reasoning and visible points to clarify.

    4. When focused outreach is particularly useful

    Active sourcing is not a replacement for every job advert. It is particularly useful when the required experience rarely appears in the inbound applicant pool, when a key role remains open, or when the internal team lacks capacity for structured market research and consistent follow-up.

    Labour-market evidence should be read carefully. Germany's Federal Employment Agency identified shortages in 157 occupational categories for 2025, while also stating that the evidence does not support a general labour shortage. Occupation, qualification and region matter. The Swiss Federal Statistical Office likewise monitors recruitment difficulty by sector and education level. A concrete vacancy therefore needs a concrete market analysis, not a blanket skills-shortage narrative.

    • A business-critical specialist or leadership role remains open despite advertising.
    • Applications arrive, but the essential experience is repeatedly missing.
    • Internal recruitment cannot sustain the necessary market research and personal follow-up.
    • Relevant profiles are usually employed in comparable roles and are not actively applying.
    • The vacancy delays projects, customer work, revenue ownership or important internal decisions.

    5. What employers should provide before the search starts

    Effective direct outreach needs more than a job description. Decision-makers should align the actual ownership, reporting line, location, principal conditions and selection process before the search begins. Reliable feedback on introduced profiles matters too.

    MuJobs uses this information to focus the market and answer candidate questions credibly. If something remains undecided, it is treated as open rather than presented as a promise. This protects the quality of the approach and makes later conversations easier.

    • A subject-matter contact with decision authority.
    • A prioritised brief rather than a long wish list.
    • Transparent information about location, working model and selection process.
    • Timely, specific feedback on introduced profiles.
    • Readiness to adjust the search in response to credible market evidence.

    6. Decision check: is the advert enough, or is an active search needed?

    • The advert has run for a reasonable period without the essential criteria appearing in the applicant pool.
    • The role is clear internally and the main conditions are aligned.
    • Relevant profiles can be narrowed to particular employers, functions or locations.
    • The company can arrange qualified conversations and feedback promptly.
    • The cost of the role remaining open outweighs the effort of structured direct outreach.

    If several points apply, the advert should not simply remain online for longer. MuJobs can structure the reachable candidate market, approach relevant people directly and build a reasoned shortlist. The first step is a short discussion about the role, the applicant market so far and the obstacles to the search.
