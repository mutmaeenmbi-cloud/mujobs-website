The best candidates are often the hardest to reach.

Why strong people disappear from traditional applicant funnels — and what changes when the search starts outside the inbox.

By MuJobs3 min readRecruitment practice

The strongest candidate for a role is not always the person actively applying. Often, they are already employed, performing well and not scrolling through job boards.

A job ad asks people to come to you. Direct search means going to the market yourself: mapping relevant companies, alternative titles and adjacent profiles, then approaching the right people with a reason to have a conversation.

Good recruitment is not about convincing everyone to leave. It is about understanding what could genuinely matter to the right person. When a role is difficult to fill, the answer is rarely simply more applications. Sometimes you need to search beyond the inbox.

01

Start with the reason someone would listen

A passive candidate is not simply an applicant who has not clicked yet. They may be satisfied with their current role, busy delivering a project or uncertain whether a move is worthwhile. The first conversation therefore has a different purpose: establishing whether the opportunity deserves attention. Explain the responsibility, the problem to solve and the room to make decisions. Generic promises about an exciting company are unlikely to answer those questions.

02

Build a market map before opening LinkedIn

For LinkedIn sourcing, start with a list of relevant business environments rather than one Boolean string. Which companies solve similar problems? Where do people gain the required responsibility? Which alternative titles describe the same work? A search for Leiter Vermarktung might also consider commercial leaders in development companies, provided their actual responsibilities match. Review profiles against the brief before making contact. A large list of names is not yet a candidate pipeline.

03

Make the first message specific and easy to answer

A useful message has three parts: why this person, why this role and what happens next. Refer to relevant experience without pretending to know more than the profile shows. Give enough context for an informed response, including the location and core responsibility. Invite a short exploratory conversation rather than asking for a full application immediately. If the person declines, respect the answer. Follow-ups should add context or close the loop, not repeat the same pressure.

04

Measure interest separately from suitability

A reply rate tells you whether a message started conversations. It does not establish whether the people can do the job. Track relevant profiles identified, replies, genuine interest, qualified conversations and interview progression separately. If people reply but decline after learning the details, the proposition may need attention. If they are interested but unsuitable, revisit the market map. This makes active sourcing a learning process, not just an outreach activity.

PUT IT INTO PRACTICE

Bring one live role to the conversation.

Take the questions in this article and apply them to the position you are trying to fill. Write down what is known, what is assumed and which conversation would resolve the most important uncertainty. A clearer brief is a practical first step towards a more relevant search.
